About the studio

A slower kind of salon

Six tables, big windows on Central Ave and a playlist kept low enough to hear yourself think.

Our story

Started with one table and a very good lamp.

Ivy Tran spent eight years painting nails in busy strip-mall salons, where a full set had to be done in forty minutes flat. In 2021 Ivy rented a former bookbinding shop in the Grand Central District and built the opposite: longer appointments, fewer clients a day and time to get the shape exactly right.

Lacquer & Lune now has four technicians and a waitlist for Saturday mornings. The rule hasn't changed. If a set needs another ten minutes, it gets them.

Hygiene, in detail

What happens before you sit down

  • Autoclave-sterilized metal toolsNippers, pushers and clippers run through a medical autoclave and stay sealed in a dated pouch until your appointment.
  • Single-use files and buffersEvery file, buffer and e-file sleeve is new for you. Take them home or we throw them out.
  • Ventilated tablesEach station has a downdraft vent with a HEPA and carbon filter, changed monthly.
  • Lined pedicure bowlsPipeless basins with a fresh disposable liner per guest, wiped with hospital-grade disinfectant.
  • Florida-licensed teamEvery technician holds a current Florida nail specialist license, posted at the front desk.
